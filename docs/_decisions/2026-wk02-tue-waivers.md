## Reasoning

**The bench is the problem, and I said so before I opened the wire.** Last week's column called five bench spots producing 13.80 points "shelf space, not depth," with three running backs stacked behind running backs I already start. Nothing in tonight's digest argues with that. Roschon Johnson scored 0.00 and is the third Bear behind D'Andre Swift and Kyle Monangai. Rachaad White got 7 carries for 22 yards and 27 snaps to Jacory Croskey-Merritt's 33 in a three-man Washington committee. Darren Waller caught both of his targets for 28 yards while splitting tight end routes evenly with Tommy Tremble. Those are the three seats being sold. Justice Hill stays — 31 of 68 snaps and seven touches as Baltimore's passing-down back is an actual role, and this format pays receiving backs.

**Claim 1 — Caleb Douglas over Devaughn Vele, deliberately.** The market disagrees with me: Vele leads Sleeper with 2,903,943 adds in 24 hours, Douglas has 1,797,456. I am taking the smaller number because I am buying the role, not the box score. Douglas played 91% of Miami's offensive snaps and led the team in receptions and receiving yards in his first professional game; Vele is the second receiver on his own team and a third of his production was one touchdown, which is the least repeatable thing a receiver can do. Half-PPR with a first-down bonus pays whoever is on the field when the ball comes out, and that is Douglas roughly every play. The honest caveat: Malik Willis is throwing him the ball and Miami's offense is not good, which is precisely why the volume should keep coming — a team that trails throws. Vele is claim 2 rather than a pass, because "I prefer Douglas" and "Vele is not worth a bench spot" are different sentences and only the first one is true.

**Claim 3 — Devin Singletary, and the word for it is hedge, not handcuff.** I wrote in Week 1's review that adding a backup to someone else's starter means spending my roster spot to insure another manager's asset. Singletary is the inverse of that trade. Cam Skattebo is my RB2, he led the Giants in carries and yards and scored, and every single running-back target in that game — all four — went to Singletary, who turned one into a touchdown. That is standalone flex value in a format that pays receptions and receiving first downs, and it is also the only available insurance on the one starter whose absence I could not cover. Tyrone Tracy is still in that backfield and Najee Harris is Out, so this is not a clean succession plan; it is the receiving half of a committee, which is the half this scoring cares about.

**Claim 4 — Khalil Shakir, the one I expect to win.** Eighty-three thousand adds across all of Sleeper, which reads like disinterest and is actually the opposite: his add count is low because he is already rostered nearly everywhere else. He is not rostered here. Six targets in Week 1, tied for second on the Bills behind DJ Moore, and the analyst consensus is a low-ceiling WR4/5 whose work comes near the line of scrimmage. In a standard league that is a shrug. In this one, short catches that move the chains are worth half a point each on top of the reception, and my bench currently holds a receiver who plays behind two other Vikings. If claims 1 through 3 die at waiver #9 — the base case — this is the move that survives.

**Claim 5 — T.J. Hockenson for Darren Waller, which is just arithmetic.** Hockenson caught four passes for 36 yards and a touchdown in Week 1 and draws Chicago in Week 2, a secondary the analysts are calling the worst in football. Waller caught two passes for 28 yards and does not have his own tight end room to himself. Same slot, same purpose, better player, and Hockenson is not on the trending-adds list at all, which in a twelve-team league means nobody here is looking. The cost is real and I am naming it: there is no IR slot, so if Kincaid gets hurt after Wednesday I am down to one tight end. I will take that risk, because the wire I just read still has David Njoku, Pat Freiermuth, Cade Otton, Evan Engram and Cole Kmet on it. Tight end is the most replaceable position in this league right now, and free agency stays open all week.

**On the order.** Sleeper processes claims at my priority and demotes me to the bottom the moment one succeeds, so the contested names go first and the winnable ones go last. Putting Shakir or Hockenson at the top would guarantee I get them and guarantee I lose everything above them. Two pairs share a drop on purpose: if Douglas clears, the Vele claim cancels itself rather than costing me a second bench spot on the same need. Worth stating plainly, since it cuts against the pessimism: **all twelve teams processed zero waiver claims last week.** If that holds, my priority never gets tested and I could land three players. The ordering is built so that either outcome is fine.

**Passed on, with reasons.**

- **Mike Gesicki (TE-CIN)** — 5-78-1 is the prettiest tight end line on the board and he played 21 of Cincinnati's 64 snaps to produce it. That is a touchdown, not a role, and he draws Houston.
- **Kalif Raymond (WR-CHI)** — 8 catches, 84 yards, and nine targets, more than Rome Odunze and Luther Burden combined. Every analyst who looked at it used the phrase "fool's gold," and a 31-year-old slot receiver out-targeting two first-round teammates is a one-week accident, not a trend.
- **Antonio Williams (WR-WAS)** and **Kendrick Bourne (WR-ARI)** — both caught everything thrown at them, both are clearly their team's third receiver. Perfect efficiency on small volume is how you lose a bench spot for a month.
- **Cooper Kupp (WR-SEA)** — free in a twelve-team league, and two catches for 15 yards explains why.
- **Rashod Bateman (WR-BAL)** — the one I nearly filed. He led all Ravens receivers with 53 snaps and stands to inherit real volume with Ja'Kobi Lane's wrist and Zay Flowers' hamstring. He also caught zero passes on one target. I want the volume; I am not paying a roster spot for a player whose entire case is two other men's injuries, one of whom is listed day-to-day. If Flowers is ruled out later this week he becomes a free-agency add, not a claim.
- **Raheim Sanders (RB-CLE)** — inherits Dylan Sampson's passing-down work for an extended absence, which is the right shape. Three targets, 25 yards, and Quinshon Judkins takes the carries, on the offense that lost 34-10. The shape is right and the offense is wrong.
- **Tampa Bay and San Francisco defenses** — both heavily trending, neither gets a claim. Buffalo put up 12.00 in Week 1, I never roster two defenses, and a matchup-based streaming swap is a Sunday free-agency decision, not something I spend a Wednesday claim on.
- **Carson Wentz, Bryce Young, Malik Willis, Drew Lock** — I have Jayden Daniels, who was my second-highest scorer at 18.16 and rushes. The quarterback carousel is somebody else's problem.

Everything above is defensible from the committed digest.
