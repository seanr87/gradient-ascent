## The shape of the league

Two numbers frame every proposal here.

**First: my starting nine is not the problem.** Gradient Ascent scored 140.46 in Week 1 against a maximum possible 140.46. I am the only team in this league that started its optimal lineup. Umojan Protectorate left 41.30 points on its bench, Sea Squirts 27.59, Three Wise Jaylens 29.30. Eleven managers lost points to their own lineup card; I lost none. That means a trade that shuffles my bench is worth almost nothing to me, and only a trade that changes who starts on Sunday is worth Sean's time.

**Second: this wire refills receivers and does not refill backs.** Of the trending free agents in this morning's digest, thirteen are wide receivers — Demarcus Robinson, Pat Bryant, Mack Hollins, Dontayvion Wicks, Malik Washington, Xavier Hutchinson, Kalif Raymond, Kendrick Bourne, Roman Wilson, Jauan Jennings, Adonai Mitchell, Antonio Williams, and a player listed as Tyreek Hill with no team beside his name. Four are running backs: Raheim Sanders, Sione Vaki, Demond Claiborne, DeeJay Dallas. Fifty-three backs and fifty-four receivers are rostered league-wide, but the replacement level is not remotely the same. Receiver is the position I can refill for free at 11:58 on a Sunday morning. Running back is not.

Put those together and the trade thesis writes itself: **trade from the position the wire replenishes into the position it doesn't, and only if it changes my lineup.** Both proposals below do exactly that. Neither is a depth move.

## Proposals

### 1. CorneliusJones (manager: CorneliusJones) — 0-1, 84.52 PF

```
GIVE: CeeDee Lamb, WR-DAL + Rachaad White, RB-WAS
GET:  Jonathan Taylor, RB-IND
```

**Why it helps me.** This is the only trade on the board that changes my Sunday lineup, and it changes exactly one slot: Lamb out, Taylor in, everything else identical. Skattebo stays at flex, Evans slides from flex to WR2, Nacua stays WR1. In a format paying half a point per rushing first down on top of 0.1 per yard, an every-down back on 250-plus carries is the single most efficient scoring shape available, and there are four running backs on the entire free-agent wire to replace one if he goes down. There are thirteen receivers. I am selling into the deep market and buying from the shallow one. It also ends the fragility I named on Tuesday and again yesterday: my backfield is currently Bucky Irving, Cam Skattebo, and a 4.80-point bench, with the Singletary hedge now sitting on my Week 2 opponent's roster.

**Why he should accept.** He is 0-1 with the second-lowest points-for in the league and four of his five bench spots occupied by Jordyn Tyson (IR), A.J. Brown (IR), Jordan Mason (IR) and Zach Charbonnet (PUP) — in a league with no IR slot, that is four roster spots scoring zero every week. Lamb is the best receiver he can acquire without dismantling his lineup, and White gives him a live second back beside Jeremiyah Love so the trade doesn't hollow out the position he's giving from. He trades one star for one star and fills a hole he cannot fill from the wire.

**Acceptance odds: fair, not good.** He may simply refuse to move his best player in Week 2, and that's a defensible answer from him. White is a body, not a sweetener, and I am saying so in the pitch rather than dressing him up — a manager who reads the transaction log knows I tried to drop him twice.

**PITCH:**

> You are 0-1 with four bench spots on IR or PUP and the second-lowest points-for in the league. I will fix the wrong half of that: CeeDee Lamb and Rachaad White for Jonathan Taylor. You get the best receiver available to you plus a live body behind Jeremiyah Love. I get the back. Neither of us is doing the other a favor, which is exactly why it works.

### 2. What can Brown do for u? (manager: dlef24) — 0-1, 108.36 PF

*Send only if proposal 1 is declined. See the note below.*

```
GIVE: Jordan Addison, WR-MIN
GET:  Alvin Kamara, RB-NO
```

**Why it helps me.** Same thesis, smaller scale. Addison is my WR4, behind Nacua, Lamb and Evans, and he scored 0.00 in Week 1 for a Minnesota offense whose quarterback is currently a trending free-agent add across Sleeper — 293,220 managers picking up Carson Wentz is the market telling me what it thinks of that passing game. Kamara is a receiving back, which is the shape this scoring pays twice: half a point for the catch, half a point again when it moves the chains. He is behind Travis Etienne in New Orleans and behind Derrick Henry and De'Von Achane on his fantasy roster, and 105,904 Sleeper managers added him in 24 hours anyway, which means somebody sees the role I see.

**Why he should accept.** He is carrying Kamara and J.K. Dobbins behind Henry and Achane, who take every meaningful touch on that roster, while fielding three wide receivers total — Nabers, Higgins, McLaurin, and nothing behind them. He also rosters two defenses, which is a spare part in a league where streaming is free. Addison is a starting NFL receiver and the insurance his lineup has none of.

**Why it's conditional.** If proposal 1 lands, my receiver room becomes Nacua, Evans and Addison, and I am not trading it down to two in the same week. If proposal 1 is declined, Addison is surplus again and this goes out. One of these, not both.

**Acceptance odds: decent.** It is a straight positional swap between two teams that each have a surplus the other lacks, and neither side is giving up a starter.

**PITCH:**

> You are carrying Alvin Kamara and J.K. Dobbins behind Derrick Henry and De'Von Achane, and three receivers total. One hamstring and you are starting a defense at flex. Jordan Addison for Kamara: you turn a back who will never see your lineup into a receiver who might, I turn a receiver who will never see mine into the depth I need. Straight across.
